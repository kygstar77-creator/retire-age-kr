How the 60% band works

For 2026/27 the standard Personal Allowance is £12,570. It goes down by £1 for every £2 of adjusted net income above £100,000, so it's zero from £125,140.

Between £100,000 and £125,140, each extra £1 is taxed at the 40% higher rate and also removes 50p of tax-free allowance, which is then taxed at 40% too. That adds up to 60p in Income Tax for each £1. Employee National Insurance of 2% on earnings above £50,270 takes it to 62%.

What this calculator leaves out

Student loan repayments, Scotland's tax bands, the High Income Child Benefit Charge and childcare support are not included. Pension amounts shown are gross. In a relief at source scheme, your pension provider claims tax relief at the basic 20% rate and adds it to your pension pot.
